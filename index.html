01The problem

Classrooms waste power even when nobody is using them.

Energy in a classroom is not wasted by the students who are there — it is wasted by the hours that follow them. The loads stay on, the room stays lit, and nobody notices.

  • A

    Loads outlive the lesson

    Lights and fans routinely remain active long after a classroom empties, drawing power into an unused space.

  • B

    Shutdown depends on memory

    Manual switching works only when somebody remembers it — at the end of a period, a busy corridor, a closing bell.

  • C

    Timers cannot understand occupancy

    A schedule knows what the clock says. It does not know whether the room is in use, partially used, or completely empty.

  • D

    Room-level automation is blunt

    Switching an entire classroom as one block treats a half-full room exactly like a full one — the empty half is powered anyway.

02The system

Meet EcoSwitch.

EcoSwitch is a supervisory layer between the classroom and its loads. It watches presence, decides whether a zone is genuinely in use, and acts only when inactivity is confirmed — zone by zone, not room by room.

Presence & occupancy sensing

Continuous presence detection combining radar presence sensing with beam-break activity, so a still room is still understood.

Zone-based detection

The classroom is divided into horizontal zones, each with its own sensing beams and its own occupancy state.

Door activity sensing

Entry and exit events give the state engine context: a door opening is activity, but it is not the same as a room being used.

Intelligent inactivity timers

Every zone runs its own timer. Any validated activity resets it, so only sustained inactivity can trigger a change.

Warning alerts

Before anything switches, the system warns the room — an audible cue that gives occupants the chance to respond.

Automated power control

Controlled loads in an inactive zone return to standby, while occupied zones keep working without interruption.

  1. 01

    Sense

    Radar presence modules and IR beam-break pairs report raw activity from each zone to the controller.

  2. 02

    Interpret

    Signals are filtered and validated before they are allowed to change a zone's occupancy decision.

  3. 03

    Timer

    Each zone tracks time since its last valid activity. Occupied zones hold the timer at zero.

  4. 04

    Warn

    Ten minutes of continued inactivity raises a warning alert through the buzzer and the local display.

  5. 05

    Control

    At fifteen minutes the zone's controlled loads are switched off — independently of every other zone.

03Spatial design

Four beams. Four zones. One room that knows the difference.

Four wall-to-wall sensing beams cut the classroom into horizontal bands of seating. Emitters on one wall pair with receivers on the opposite wall, and each beam guards one band. Interrupt a beam and that zone reports activity. Leave the band quiet and its own inactivity timer starts.

04Sensing strategy

Two sensing approaches. One objective: know when the room is truly in use.

Presence and activity are different questions. EcoSwitch answers both, using one technology for continuous presence and another for deterministic, row-level activity.

Approach A · presence

mmWave presence sensing

A 24 GHz FMCW radar front end measures movement and micro-movement in the space. That makes it useful for continuous occupancy detection rather than pure motion triggering.

  • Can detect moving and relatively stationary people
  • Well suited to continuous occupancy detection
  • Does not require optical line-of-sight
  • Does not depend on visible movement alone
Watch-outs
  • Placement and environmental reflections affect accuracy
  • Static objects can produce radar reflections that must be handled
  • Requires calibration for the space it serves
  • More complex to implement than a simple beam-break sensor
Approach B · activity

IR / laser beam-break

An emitter projects a beam across the room to a receiver on the opposite wall. Interrupt the beam and the zone reports activity — one clean digital signal per sensing line.

  • Beam interruption directly indicates activity
  • Extremely simple and inexpensive per zone
  • Works well for fixed seating and defined rows
  • Predictable, easy to reason about and test
Watch-outs
  • Requires line-of-sight and precise alignment
  • A single beam-break cannot confirm continued presence once the beam is clear again
  • Rigid furniture or layout changes can disrupt alignment
  • Ambient light and dust can affect a bare optical receiver

For fixed seating → an IR beam-break can be extremely deterministic: one row, one beam, one clear answer.

For continuous presence → mmWave is more flexible: it keeps sensing when people are still and silently present.

Neither approach is universally superior. EcoSwitch uses them together: radar for continuous presence, beams for deterministic zone activity.

05Control logic

Fifteen quiet minutes is all it takes.

Inactivity is treated as a countdown, not a guess. Every validated sign of occupancy resets the timer to zero; only sustained silence is allowed to change a zone's power state.

  4. 15:00

    The zone's controlled loads return to standby. Other zones are untouched, and sensing continues so the zone can wake immediately.

Safety note In a real installation, essential safety circuits — emergency lighting, alarms and exit signage — should remain outside the automated shutdown system entirely.

07Data interpretation

The sensors never touch the power.

A beam interruption is not a decision. It is evidence. Between the sensor and the relay sits a pipeline that filters noise, decides occupancy, tracks time and only then issues a control action.

  1. 01Raw sensor inputBeam state, radar presence, door activity
  2. 02FilteringDebounce, hold time, rejection of single-frame glitches
  3. 03Occupancy decisionCombined evidence becomes a per-zone yes / no
  4. 04Zone stateOccupied, idle, warning or powered down
  5. 05TimerEach zone counts its own quiet time
  6. 06Control actionRelay command for that zone's loads — and nothing else

08Hardware

The hardware behind the decision.

A short, deliberately conservative bill of materials. Component roles are described by function rather than claimed specification, because the final selection depends on the installation.

Controller

ESP32-S3

Reads every sensor input, runs the occupancy and timer logic, and drives the buzzer, display and relay outputs. Its wireless capability is what lets a dashboard attach later without new hardware.

Presence sensing

S3KM1110 mmWave sensor

A compact 24 GHz FMCW radar module used for continuous presence detection — including people who are in the room but almost completely still.

Beam source

IR emitter

Projects an infrared beam across the room to its paired receiver on the opposite wall. Its job is simple by design: produce one stable, uninterrupted line per zone.

Beam detection

Photodiode / phototransistor receiver

Senses the beam arriving at the far wall. Losing that signal is the activity event that tells the controller a zone's beam has been crossed.

Secondary trigger

PIR sensor

A low-cost passive infrared motion cue used as a cross-check. Excellent at noticing movement instantly, but unsuitable on its own for confirming that someone stayed.

Local warning

Buzzer

The audible cue at the ten-minute mark. It gives occupants a chance to respond before the automated power control step, which is what keeps the system feel fair rather than abrupt.

Local status

OLED display

Shows each zone's state, the timer currently running and the active power state. Invaluable during setup, alignment and calibration — and for demonstrating the logic without a laptop.

Load switching

Relay module

An isolated switching stage between the controller and the loads it is allowed to control. In the prototype it drives low-voltage demonstration loads only.

This page describes component roles and function, not a verified electrical design. Ratings, protection and wiring must be specified for the installation by a qualified professional.

09Zoning

Why switch the whole classroom when only one zone is empty?

Room-level control is a single switch for a space that is rarely used as a single space. Zoning lets the room respond to where the people actually are.

10Safety & deployment

Automation has to respect the room it manages.

Switching classroom power automatically is an engineering responsibility, not a software one. EcoSwitch is designed so that the control logic is independent of the electrical safety design.

Prototype runs low-voltage loads

The demonstration build switches low-voltage loads. It is built to prove behaviour and logic, not to be installed as-is.

Mains switching needs rated equipment

Real classroom mains switching requires properly rated contactors, protective devices, enclosures and wiring, matched to the circuits they serve.

The controller does not switch mains directly

The ESP32-S3 drives a control stage. It is never the component that opens or closes a mains circuit.

Life-safety circuits stay live

Emergency lighting, alarms, exit signage and other essential safety systems should not be part of an automated shutdown system at all.

Qualified installation, verified handover

A real deployment should be designed and installed by a qualified electrician, then verified before anyone relies on it.

Placement and calibration decide accuracy

Reflections, furniture layout and beam alignment change what the sensors report. Calibration on site is part of the installation, not an afterthought.

Design principle

EcoSwitch controls only what is safe to control. Anything the room depends on for safety — lighting for egress, alarms, signage — belongs to an independent circuit that automation cannot and should not switch.

The controller already speaks Wi-Fi, the state engine is already per zone, and the data already exists. Everything below is an extension of the same chain — not a rebuild.
